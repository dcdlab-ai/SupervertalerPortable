## THIS MONTH IN HISTORY BATTLE ASCALON

AUGUST 12, 1099

Often considered the last action of the First Crusade, this took place after the capture of Jerusalem and a 10,200-strong Crusader army led by Godfrey of Bouillon defeated and drove off a Fatimid army of Egypt. At dawn on 12 August, the Crusader army launched a surprise attack on the Fatimid army who were still sleeping in their camp outside the defensive walls of Ascalon The Crusaders quickly defeated the Fatimid infantry, while the Fatimid cavalry made little contribution in the fighting. The battle ended in less than an hour and the Crusaders butchered captives en masse. The Vizier fled by ship to Egypt and estimates of 12,700 Fatimid dead were recorded. Ascalon, however, became a base of operations for invasions of the Kingdom of Jerusalem for many years.

## INTRODUCTION

This game grew out of a confluence of factors. I'm familiar with Clontarf and live reasonably near the site of the battle, though to be fair, there is a considerable amount of dispute about where the actual battle took place. The general consensus is that it occurred about the Ballybough Bridge, but much like the site of the battle of Hastings, opinions differ. Secondly, I have been telling the Kinchlets gory stories about the battle of Clontarf for ages and a game seemed appropriate. But lastly, I got the chance to pick up a small collection of Vikings and Dark Ages Irish. ## SEND  THREE  AND  FOURPENCE

But mainly, I just wanted to play a game and I didn't want to have to wait until I had the right number of figures or the right rules or whatever. I'm not sure the Dark Ages is going to be a big period for me, but I don't see any harm in just going at it bald headed and making do with whatever I've got. You can just, as the Internet keeps telling me, just do things.

Last month, we covered some of the background of the battle of Clontarf, the sort of potted history the editor hates (no I don't! I think it's generally essential when accompanying a scenario unless it's a battle almost everyone knows... Ed .), but in brief...

## HISTORY

The Vikings founded and then subsequently ruled Dublin and were bad (boo!). Then the High King of Ireland, Brian Boru, (hurrah!) marched down to Dublin and fought them at Clontarf. The Irish won, no doubt due to their pure hearts and manly virtues, and put an end to the Vikings' filthy heathen ways. Brian Boru had his head chopped off by a Viking straggler after the battle, which was sad.

This is sufficient historical knowledge to play these scenarios.

## MINIATURES &amp; TERRAIN

- ◗ Three Irishmen &amp; a dog (the dog does not necessarily need to be Irish, but it helps)
- ◗ 15 Viking figures
- ◗ 8-10 Irish figures.
- ◗ 5 tents
- ◗ 1 nun
- ◗ 1 lady Viking
- ◗ 1 other dog (nationality unimportant)
- ◗ Some markers to distinguish wounded Vikings
- ◗ Some trees and bushes
- ◗ A fire

## SCENARIO: BE NOT DISMAYED

Maundy Thursday, outside of the Dublin 1014: the High King Brian Boru has brought an army to Dublin to defeat the pagan Danes and their lackeys from Leinster. Tomorrow will be Good Friday and two great armies will clash outside of Dublin. It will be a mighty contest and one which will decide the fate of the kingdom.

![Image](/root/duckling/outputs/cee9f5ee-3cd8-4857-a1b7-e46711aa6981/images/image_000033_b9837c900f0afaf6f0ff256d8d760a00c1f90f78e550e5d432cdedd65b5533e7.png)

Word has come that there is dissension in the ranks of the Danes and that some of their leaders have little stomach for the fight. A nun living in the city has learned where their shieldwall will be weakest and has snuck out of the city, but was unable to get to the Irish lines. She is currently in the Viking camp and must be brought out. She is hiding in one of the Viking tents. She will tie a red cord around the entrance to the tent. She will answer to the name of Rahab.

The warriors will have to sneak into the camp and get Rahab out.

## SET-UP

This scenario is played on a 2.5' x 2.5' table. Put a circle of small stones in the centre of the board: this is the central fire. A few pieces of scatter (pigs on spits, barrels, unconscious drunken vikings, etc) should be placed around the camp for visual interest and to break the playing area up a little bit. There should be five tents placed around the fire in a rough circle about eight inches away from the fire.. Around the edges of the table place trees and shrubs about 3-4 inches in.

The warriors may deploy anywhere in the woods. They do not necessarily have to be deployed together. Place two Vikings within two inches of the fire and three Vikings adjacent to three randomly determined tents: no more than one Viking per tent.

## SPECIAL RULES

What shall we do with the drunken Viking? As any fool knows, the Vikings at Clontarf all fought with massive hangovers, and these boys are no exception. All the Vikings in the camp have been hitting the mead fairly hard and will move accordingly to the following rules.

- ◗ If there is an identified enemy within LOS, they will move towards that enemy and attack.
- ◗ If there is combat happening, but out of their LOS, they will make a half move towards the combat and then make the rest of their move randomly.

![Image](/root/duckling/outputs/cee9f5ee-3cd8-4857-a1b7-e46711aa6981/images/image_000034_a5f6214326b0c1b5c5e053bcda905ef69bf7ab76ba10994596b669a3757b970e.png)

## Knowing me, knowing you (I should resist saying Ah ha... Ed .)

Given that there are Irish on both sides at Clontarf, it will be a little difficult for the Vikings to tell friend from foe in the dark. Any Irish warrior involved in combat will be identified as an enemy. If a Viking

moves within six inches of an Irish warrior, he should make a Will (TN12) roll. On a success, they will have spotted the warrior.

If an Irish warrior engages in combat with a Viking and can finish the fight in one turn (i.e. strike causing sufficient damage to kill his opponent) AND there isn't a Viking within LOS, the warrior is not immediately identified as an enemy. Once an Irish warrior is identified as an enemy, all Vikings know he's an enemy and he will never lose that status. I tried writing a rule to allow for some Vikings knowing and some not, it was awkward, took ages and added precious little to the game in playtest. If you think you can make that work (maybe by using a GM?), be my guest.

![Image](/root/duckling/outputs/cee9f5ee-3cd8-4857-a1b7-e46711aa6981/images/image_000035_0216376871043f45de99863170ee3a2fdb2cdf7790c17171361b92224df455c3.png)

- ◗ If there is no combat happening, they will move randomly, but the final two inches of their move will be towards the fire.

## The flickering flame

The only light source on the table is the fire lit in the centre of the camp. Each turn roll 2d6 and add 2. That is the number of inches (measured from the centre of the fire) that there is clear LOS. Outside this pool of light, figures can only see for six inches (measured in all directions). The Irish warriors can always move freely, but the movement of the drunken Vikings is affected by their LOS.

## PARA DEPLOYMENT

To simulate the drop, cut a small piece of paper for each figure in a unit marking clearly which piece represents the NCO/Officer. Place the pieces of paper on a ruler and hold the ruler at least 24' (preferably more) over the table above the intended drop zone. Tip the pieces off the ruler so they flutter down onto the table. On turn one, two of the main paratrooper units arrive along with one MMG team. On turn two, the HQ, the remaining paratrooper unit and the MMG team arrive. If a paratrooper unit lands within 6' of German troops then they immediately enter close combat.

## ACTIONS &amp; OUTCOMES

Paratroopers should attempt to rally around their officer or NCO. If a paratrooper lands on the burning houses they are killed. If they land on the roof of the church they are stuck there for the rest of the game, but they may fire. If they land on a house, then they must make their way through the house to the ground floor to exit the building.

## SAINT MERE EGLISE TERRAIN

By this stage Saint Mere Eglise had suffered a fair bit of damage and several of the buildings were in ruins. Before the American deploy, choose two of the blocks of housing in the town and count these as being ruins rather than complete houses. The Americans may hide anti-tank guns in ruins if they wish. The fields that are illustrated on the map as having crops should provide infantry with some cover and also make spotting/ lines of sight a little more difficult. Your ruleset should be able to cope with that, but as a rule of thumb if the spotter is on the same level as the enemy hiding in the crop field they can only spot them at a distance of 6'. If the spotter is in an intact building and looking out of a window or on a roof, then this can be 18'.

Some of the fields are muddy rather than full of crops. The only impact this has is to reduce the speed of infantry and AFV, but trucks cannot cross these fields . There are also numerous hedgerows in the vicinity, these are marked with a green line. Note that some of the houses have gardens which are surrounded by hedges too.

## THE FAILED COUNTERATTACK

After the initial paratrooper drop on Saint Mere Eglise, the German garrison withdrew and 505th took the town with little opposition. Even the main attack was a fiasco, it appears to have been badly coordinated. By early afternoon it was clear that American armour had broken out of the beachhead and was closing on Saint Mere Eglise. The German attack was stopped in its tracks before the first tanks of Task Force Raff rattled into town followed by a pair of battalions of the 8th Infantry Regiment. Shortly after 1800, paratroopers supported by a pair of tanks hit the flank of one of the German battalions. They quickly folded and 160 men were captured. This took the German POW haul to around 700 with another 400 killed.

## SET-UP

Set up the table with suitable terrain that is agreed upon by both players in terms of placement and what effects the terrain has on troops. As the scenario does not specifically mention any terrain set up requirements, the rulebook suggestion of one piece of terrain in each quarter of the board seems fair.

Also, as per the rulebook (see page 184), one unit is designated as the Defender and the other as the Attacker . The Defender is the warband that is escorting the white horse to the Druid Stones; in this case it is Rhodri's warband.

Roll a die or best of three rounds of Rock, Paper, Scissors to determine who will play which army. Whoever is determined to be the Defender , Rhodris' warband, chooses which table edge to deploy along and the Attacker will then deploy opposite.

## SPECIAL RULES

The Defender must now write down which of their units is actually escorting the white horse. It can be assumed that the baggage trains with each other unit causes Grimnir to question which unit is actually defending the horse. It is only when the unit written down is removed from play that the owning player reveals that it held the white horse. The horse cannot be swapped between units, and if it happens to be in the last remaining unit of Rhodri's warband left on the table then the game automatically stops and Glory Points are awarded to determine the victor.

Play ends when the horse escorting unit is destroyed or routed; no Attacking units are left in play; or, as above, only the horse escorting unit is left in play on the Defending side.

Victory conditions: The Attacker gains 5 Glory for destroying or routing the unit with the white horse. The Defender gains 4 Glory if the white horse escorting unit is still there at the end of the battle. Each side also gains 1 Glory for each enemy unit destroyed or routed prior to the game ending.

## TALLY HO CHAPS!

Amiens Part 1: A Black Day for the German Army. With pictures from a game by Toutatis at Crisis 2009 Words and photos by Jon Sutherland

## INTRODUCTION

Over the next couple of months I wanted to look at a much neglected wargame period, the First World War or the Great War. Understandably it's hard to get enthused about slug-fest massacres and mud-coloured tabletop scarred with pock mark craters and laced with trench lines full of jumbled up figures. It doesn't have to be like this and we have looked at some alternative theatres in the past and extolled the virtues of the early war and its fluid movement and cavalry. I want to look at two campaigns in 1918, both of which contributed in different ways to Germany's defeat. This month, we'll examine options for a 'Black day for the German army' and next month, the Germans will be on the front foot with their Ludendorff Offensive. Chronologically, they are the wrong way around, but... I wanted to introduce some game ideas here, so please forgive me!

## BACKGROUND

On August 8 1918, the British launched the Hundred Days Offensive. It was an attack that would directly lead to the defeat of Germany. Whilst often portrayed as a British operation, it involved French, Australian and Canadian troops. Significantly it both caught the Germans by surprise and was the first time that the Western Front stalemate was shattered by combined arms. It is notable for the novel use of infantry, artillery, tanks and aircraft. The attack punched through the German lines and inflicted heavy losses on the enemy.

## THE GAME

The game is figure and scale agnostic, so you could play in 15mm, 28mm or larger. As such all movement and ranges are given as paces, and you can decide if 1 pace is 1cm or 1inch depending on the scale of figures you are using. ## COVER &amp; PROTECTION

In Blast Off there are two separate types of obstacles which may improve your chance of survival Cover and Protection.

## COVER

This represents something which obscures the target and offers limited defence. It might be a cloud of smoke or vapour, scrub, a flimsy barricade or a pile of clutter. A character is classed as being in or behind cover if they are within 1 pace of it. In some cases, both the shooter and the target can claim to be behind cover if they are either side of it and within 1 pace - for example they might be in or behind a cloud of smoke or vapour!

## PROTECTION

This represents something which provides a solid defence from injury. It might be a wall, bulkhead, a vehicle or a stack of solid metal containers. A character is classed as being behind Protection if they are within 1 pace of it.

However, you also need to consider how they might be using the Cover or Protection. For example if a character is firing around Cover or Protection using a weapon with their right hand and firing to their right, they have to lean out to do so thus exposing more of their body as a potential target. In such cases Cover gives no bonus in defence and the defence bonus from Protection drops from 2 down to 1. The same rule applies if a target is more then 1 pace from the Cover or Protection. In all cases use your common sense and if in doubt roll 1 dice, and on Evens the bonus applies, odds it does not.

## THE BASIC RULES

- ◗ The game is played by 2 or more players with the aim of killing or forcing the other player's characters to flee and thus be the last Star Hunter standing.
- ◗ Each player picks a sheet on which there are a duo of Sci-Fi Characters: a Star Hunter and a Co-Pilot
- ◗ At the start of a blast off each player draws 1 card at random from either the Skills Deck or the Edge Deck and then allocates that card to one of their characters.

![Image](/root/duckling/outputs/cee9f5ee-3cd8-4857-a1b7-e46711aa6981/images/image_000081_7735520e6e3f67cf901a2b03493c9e7c8a878420c5d9b15f16bd4c2063f5d9af.png)

- ◗ The playing cards or, if you print them yourself, the Character Cards for each duo are then placed together into an Action Pack along with a Red and Black Joker and then shuffled before turning the pack face down.
- ◗ One phase 1 turn over the cards 1 by 1 and as each character's card is drawn you must state which one of the permitted actions on their character sheet you wish them to carry out
- ◗ Having announced the chosen action roll the character's Action Dice (or their Reduced Dice) to see if they are successful.
- ◗ You may choose to Hold instead of acting, waiting for the right moment to act. Then when you wish to act, roll to react and if successful your character will act before their opponent.
- ◗ If the Red Joker is drawn this is Good Luck and whichever character's card is drawn next may carry out their action automatically without rolling their dice.
- ◗ If the Black Joker is drawn this is Bad Luck and whichever character's card is drawn next can make no action on that turn.
- ◗ If successful in making a shot or fighting, roll the character's Action Dice or Reduced Action Dice to see if they hit.
- ◗ Once all of the cards have been turned over, remove any cards the characters may have lost due to wounds, then reshuffle the pack placing the red and black joker back into it and then begin the next phase.

## THE BLAST OFF

- ◗ The Blast Off begins with both duos standing 20 paces apart on the street of a frontier settler town on a distant planet, the corridor on a space station or derelict space ship or something similar and the players must move their characters towards each other until the shooting starts when they can be moved as necessary. I would suggest that your street or the corridor is at least 12 paces wide to allow for lateral movement.
- ◗ Each character begins with their weapon holstered or by their side and it has to be 'Drawn' before it can be used.
- ◗ No character may leave the street or corridor or enter a building or room but they may use the cover or protection available such as parked vehicles, crates, containers, piles of junk, etc. I would suggest that you place at least 6 pieces of such scatter terrain around the street or corridor; your characters may well need to use it!
- ◗ As each character takes hits they will lose 1 or more cards and when they have no cards left they are unable to carry on, being 'Hors du Combat' or critically wounded. Thus as your characters become wounded they will be able to undertake less actions.
- ◗ If a character fails a Courage Test then on their next action they test to recover their nerve and if successful may then perform an action on that turn.
- ◗ When the Blast Off has ended, the winning player may remain in play with all of their wounds/cards restored.
- ◗ They can also draw another card at random from either the Skills Deck or the Edge Deck and then allocate that new card to one of their characters. Thus one character might have two cards or each character have one each. They continue to do this choosing new Trait or Edge cards at random until they win the overall Blast Off or are eliminated.

Words by Alun Stuart and photographs by John Treadaway of the Rosebud game by the Barnsley Association of Wargamers Custer's last stand game at Partizan 2021

W e all know about the demise of Custer at Little Bighorn, but many of us forget that this was part of a much larger operation. Under instruction from Brigadier-General Alfred H. Terry, the veteran frontier commander General George Crook marched out of Fort Fetterman, Wyoming on May 29 1876. Terry's plan was to trap the Sioux and the Cheyenne in the Big Horn Mountains and eliminate them as a frontier threat.

Crook's men made pretty slow progress and were under constant observation by the Sioux. His column reached the Prairie Dog Creek near the Tongue River on June 7. Two days later Little Hawk and some of his Cheyenne warriors attempted to stampede some of Crook's horses. The attempt failed, but spooked Crook who then fell back to Goose Creek. On June 14, the promised Crow and Shoshone allies arrived (around 175 Crow and 86 Shoshone). On June 16 Crook opted to push on without a supply column. Each man carried a hundred rounds, four days' rations and a blanket.

Crook's intention was to find the main enemy encampment. He did not know that it was located on Rosebud Creek and there were around a thousand warriors facing him. The key enemy commander belonged to the Oglala Sioux, Crazy Horse. He lead a supremely confident force, Sitting Bull, a leading holy man had seen victory in a vision.

## GAMING ROSEBUD

Although this engagement looks great in 28mm, smaller scale encounters really give you a better understanding of relatively small forces operating in a vast open country without secure flanks or rear. Units can quickly find themselves very much on their own, isolated and vulnerable with little hope of reinforcement.

One of the big problems in wargaming the Plains War is the apparent lack of strategy from the natives and the rather rudimentary communications between the elements of the US forces. A great way of playing a game like this is to assign different players to each of the tribes (or at least a collection of tribes) and split up the US commands so that each player has slightly different objectives. At least for the US forces all of the orders emanating from Crook should be filtered through an umpire. The umpire should deliberately garble some of the orders so that misunderstandings and incorrect moves are made. Each of the sub commanders should also be given a degree of freedom to interpret orders and to ensure that their commands are not overrun.

## VICTORY

Deciding on victory conditions is also quite problematic. Clearly it was not Crook's intention to bring the natives to battle on his own, rather he was seeking their village and intended to destroy it. It seems fairly obvious that the native forces were focused on preventing him from doing this. This could give us the foundation on our victory conditions. If Crook is able to fend off the native warrior attacks and ensure that the bulk of his commands remain intact then there is no reason to assume that his next move would not be to move on the village and destroy it. This would constitute a victory. For the native warriors they simply need to bloody Crook's troops sufficiently in order to deter him from continuing to march north toward the village.

## INTRODUCTION

I don't like my war games to be too predictable and like the friction of command and control to be reflected in my games and rule sets. Many people use a card driven system or draw a dice from my bag, as used in Bolt Action . Other variants include action cards which determine the order in which troops move, fight or shoot. Some players prefer a simple IGO UGO system where both sides move all of their forces, before passing the baton to their opponent, with firing order determined in the same way.

I have used all of these systems in the past, and all raise issues. My group nearly always plays in multiplayer games with three or more a side and with large Words and Photos by Dave Tuck .

numbers of units under each players command. We like uncluttered tables, accepting small dice as casualty markers, tape measures and a quick reference sheet as necessary evils.

## OPTIONS

A simple IGO UGO system does work at this level, but is bland and gives none of the unpredictability of real warfare. It also leaves one side uninvolved for large portions of the game, leaving players drifting off for coffee or comfort breaks. Adding a system like in Lion Rampant where a target number must be rolled before each activation, works, but with twenty or more units under each command in our games, that is a lot of dice rolling! It can also be incredibly frustrating when you fail your first order, and have to wait for your allies who have much luckier rolls to continue, until they all inevitably fail.

Card systems increase the amount of table clutter and are another ancillary item that must be generated, and brought along to the games. It may also need to be rejigged if force composition changes, from game to game.

Having said that, action cards work in some systems, and we have used this in our Renaissance period games. A deck of four move and three fire and three melee cards are generated, with a single re-shuffle card added in. A card is turned and all troops can carry out that action. The next card is drawn and that action say fi re - is an option for all troops. Troops that have fired are marked with a small puff of cotton wool. The next fire card to be turned over allows them to reload, but not fire. This carries on until the reshuffle card is drawn, when the cards are shuffled and it starts again.

![Image](/root/duckling/outputs/cee9f5ee-3cd8-4857-a1b7-e46711aa6981/images/image_000105_c7935bc6955ab1aa8cd6d1a5a559b9dbf15a77bcf4b853ab378ebf57a78101d0.png)

It is possible in this system that multiple moves may occur before any firing, and that troops may be in contact and shaping up to each other for a while before any casualties occur, as the melee card has yet to be drawn. Conversely, three rounds of melee may occur consecutively. This gives a completely unpredictable nature to the battles. If move cards come up, reinforcements can be moved in quickly. or the melee may be over before a commander can react. That all seems pretty accurate to me. It does however, take a lot of control away from the commanders, and makes them more of a reactor to events, rather than a controller of them. That may be too much for some players.

## HOW DOES IT WORK THEN?

It is a system of IGO UGO where each turn is broken down into phases, determined by how many dice there are in each string, and the order you choose to use them in.

Players establish how many dice they will be using, in our games it is usually one dice per unit , battery or general . The sweet spot a something between 12 and 18 dice.

Players then roll the dice and sort them into the respective numbers rolled. All the ones, all the twos etc. They then compare the number of ones rolled, to the number of sixes and if there are more ones than sixes, they hand the ones over to the opposing side for that turn only, for them to reroll and add to their strings. Ones are always removed whether the above applies or not, but sixes are always retained even if there are more ones than sixes.

So a player may have three sixes four fives four fours etc. Both sides then dice for initiative and the winner start by declaring which string they will spend. They can choose any string: a long string of say three, fives, or a single string of one, two for example.

They can spend the dice in a a string as follows:

- ◗ One dice allows a unit to move or manoeuvre or mount or dismount. Any or all dice can be used on the same unit to carry out these actions, or you could limit it to no more than two or three actions per unit if that suits you better. A string of three dice would allow a unit of dragoons to mount up, move a normal turn and dismount at the end of it. As firing is free (but only once a turn: see below for an exception ) they can fire as well, but will count as moving. Hopefully you can see just how flexible a system this is!
- ◗ It can be used to declare a charge.
- ◗ It can be used to remove a disorder or attempt to rally or reform a unit.
- ◗ Two dice from the same string enables a special move to be made. This can be a forced march (in our rules this adds 50% to the normal moves) or one can 'brigade move' where three or four units can be moved together providing they are acting in concert and doing the same action.

## SIX OF THE BEST

Sixes have special uses too.

- ◗ They can be changed to any other number to extend a string to allow extra actions in that phase.
- ◗ They can be used to obtain a reroll of any failed action of one dice. A firing dice, melee dice or morale roll for example.
- ◗ In some periods (Ancients, Wars of the Roses, 30 Years War) depending upon the rule set we use, a six can be used to give a firing or melee addition to a unit. This confers a plus one to the firing or melee factor, and is poetically called 'Loose!' for firing or 'Unleash the Dogs of War' for the melee bonus!

- ◗ A six can be used with any other dice in a string to fire a unit again in a turn, but not in the same phase.
- ◗ Finally a six can be carried over to the next turn, reducing the number of dice you roll at the start of that turn.

## ENHANCING RULES

The system is so easy to use and so flexible that any items not covered can easily be added. It also can be added to existing rules to substitute the system, if any, already in place. We have recently played a lot of ACW games using rules written in 1990, called Whipping

## F'RINSTANCE

In this example we have 15 dice representing 15 individual units. 2 units represented by the red dice are Grenadier/veteran units, two units represented by the green dice are militia/ poor units, and a black dice represent regular quality troops. Those units per dice can be, one infantry battalion, one skirmish unit, one cavalry squadron, or one artillery battery.

Having rolled the dice they have ended up as below in the photo. Arrange them into 'strings' ie same numbered dice. Remember that if you roll a red dice and it rolls a 1 you may reroll that dice once to attempt to get a higher score on the dice. If you roll a six on a green dice, it cannot be used as a six, but you may select a lower number on the dice to complement any other number on any of the other dice strings. After the dice have been rolled and put into their 'strings' the colour of the dice no

longer matters.

As you can see, there is an equal number of 1's and 6's that have been rolled, so in this instance the 1's are not passed over to the opponent for them to roll to see if they can make use of them.

On your first dice string turn, you select any one of your dice strings, for example the 3x 4 string, to use as orders for three units. Or 2 dice together to enable the same action for a whole Brigade and one remaining dice to use as an order for an individual unit. When you have completed your actions with your three dice it passes over to your opponent for them to action a dice string of their choice and so on until all the dice strings have been used up, then the second turn begins and all the dice are re rolled, Note, that any sixes in your possession from the previous turn(s) if not used may be retained moving forward.

Using the same outline system for most of the many periods we play, doesn't half speed the game along!

## ARE YOU REALLY IN CHARGE?

Commands degrading as they take losses is also important in this system. Every time a force loses a unit, or general, they also lose the dice which came with it. Furthermore, the system is great for historical refights: we recently refought Austerlitz, and - on any turn that the French won initiative - we allowed them an extra four command dice. We christened this the 'Napoleon effect'!

In another Napoleonic refight in the Peninsula, we needed to reflect the fact that the Spanish troops were of poor quality. We achieved this by replacing their command dice with a different colour dice for each poor quality unit, and if those dice rolled a six they were ignored, and substituted with any roll between two and five and added to that string. This gave them far less flexibility and none of the special factors allowed to other armies. We also decided to reduce their dice pool by two or three dice, to reflect their poor command structure and ability to motivate their forces.

Conversely, we allow any Elite troops, (again recognised by a different colour dice) to reroll any ones and count the rerolled number, for the 'more ones than sixes' rule.

Finally. we reflected the early gunpowder period and its slow rate of fire by changing one of the usual rules. In this period it does cost you to fire muskets and arquebus. It is amazing what a simple change like this has on the game, and it allows pikes to have a proper function on the Italian War or Thirty Years war battlefield.

## CONCLUSION

So: why should you resort to dice strings? Well, I hope I have demonstrated how flexible a system it is, using D6 which I am pretty sure every gamer will have a stack of.

It allows our multiplayer games to flow a lot faster, particularly when there is a quieter sector, when the players can process their actions at their own pace. We often find that for large parts of a game it develops into two or three separate mini battles, and it stops players having to wait while their colleagues or opponents move all of their forces. Everyone coordinates at the end of each turn, and roll the next turns dice at the same time. The random number of dice in each string and the order you choose to use them keeps these games punchy and interesting.

In our Austerlitz game, I, as the Austrian Commander, had to reinforce part of my line quickly. I had two strings of four left which allowed me to move a Brigade twice on each of two separate turns, whilst my opponent, who had used his long strings earlier in the turn, could only respond in a piecemeal way, with his two and one dice strings he had kept back. There is a lot more tactical thought to the games using this system.

![Image](/root/duckling/outputs/cee9f5ee-3cd8-4857-a1b7-e46711aa6981/images/image_000110_25f3493f847b7ce075ac2fa5ccd0712b199b2051bfa61d5c82eac9c44c786c47.png)

Once you have established the basic options for your choices with each dice type - twos to fives, or sixes you rarely have to look back at the rules and can add period specific tweaks, like 'Dogs of War' or 'Loose!'. It is very easy to reflect troop quality and class with the different coloured dice variant, to increase or reduce the chance of sixes, or the dreaded ones! The same thing applies with the Napoleon effect, allowing one side the chance to get extra movement options.

We also like the way your army gradually degrades and becomes paralyzed the more units you lose. The removal of a dice for each unit lost or destroyed is so easy and simple to apply, and really does have an effect on those strings and their lengths.

Finally, and while I am not a solo gamer, it allows you to fairly play a game where you can manage and represent both sides, without the usual bias that often occurs when you favour one side more than the other. It also works seamlessly with Gemini or other AI programs. If you download your rule set into it and ask it for options for one or both sides you will be amazed by the results.

I hope this article has been of interest and has at least given you food for thought, and made you consider why you use the command and control system you do. If you give this system a try I hope you will be impressed and get far better games in the future. We have. Roll on Thursday!

E arlier this year I was asked by a good friend if I would paint a resin model he had purchased in a style similar to some of the scratch built wargame terrain he had previously purchased from me. The model is a resin windill produced by Grand Manner but is now no longer in production. I was given free reign as to the colours used so long as it fitted in with the earlier Peninsular wargame terrain that I had painted for him (I suspect this is simply a devilish plan by Mr Harwood to exhaust my store of Windy Miller/Trumpton quips.... but I digress! Ed .)

In addition I could if I wanted re-base the windmill. I used a fretsaw to remove large pieces of the resin base and after cutting a new base from some 3mm thick plastic card I superglued the windmill on to the new circular base. I have used ready-mixed filler, Milliput two part epoxy putty and DAS modelling clay to sculpt the new base, more in keeping with the pieces of terrain that he had earlier purchased from me. Additional texture was applied with sieved stones and sand over PVA glue and then 'sealed' with dilute PVA glue to which I have added a tiny drop of washing-up liquid.

Prior to starting any work on this model I had already washed it in warm soapy water to ensure a model free of release agent or grease. I have used both traditional model paints and craft paints to build up the base colours before adding subtle tints, darker to the base of the model and lighter on the upper surfaces. In addition light 'washes' were used to define the beautiful sculped detail. The exposed stones were next and these were picked out in grey to which I have added a touch of Sepia just to add some warmth.

The groundwork was painted in my usual Snakebite Leather colour (a tester pot mixed up for me at my local DIY store) and then drybrushed with a Snakebite/Titanium White mix. Individual stones were picked out in grey, highlighted white before an another wash of Flesh Tone and Strong Tone were used to define detail.

The terracotta tiles were painted in a wide variety of different colours over a Linen craft paint base and when finished the roof was both drybrushed with a lighter colour and given a wash of dirty grey. Detail painting was done with a fine sable mix brush. The sails were painted with a mix of Linen and Light Cream with Titanium White highlights while Barbarian Brown and Sepia were used for the wooden parts before matt varnishing with a mix of Galleria matt varnish and matt ModPodge. The final piece of the puzzle was to add some dyed sawdust and ground foam over PVA glue and odd tufts of static grass tufts and ground foam over superglue.

## H O B B Y   T I P S

## 1. PREPPING THE KIT

I had been asked if I would paint this resin windmill in a similar style to some scratch built terrain that a friend had purchased from me some time ago. The windmill was produced by Grand Manner but is no longer in production.

The model was cast in a light 'putty' coloured resin (that I find just a little too brittle), in four parts: the main structure, the conical roof, the sails and a small dowel which allowed for the sails to revolve.

## 2. BASICS

I had been given free reign as to the colours I used and also given the choice of using the existing base or sculpting a new one. I choose to re-base the model as I thought the main structure was a little off-balance and placed to one side.

The new base was cut from 3mm thick plastic card and the windmill superglued in place. The new groundwork was scrulpted from ready-mixed filler, Milliput epoxy putty and DAS modelling clay.

## 3. ROUGH STUFF

Additional ground texture was added from sieved stones, sand and static grass applied over PVA glue.

I later sealed the base with dilute PVA glue to which I have added a tiny drop of washing-up liquid which helps to break the surface tension and keep all of that fine texure in place ready for painting.

## 4. STONE ME

I used both modelling paint and craft paints to build up a light cream or off white wall colour making sure to have darker cream or Sepia colour on the lower edges and lighter (almost pure White ) on the upper walls.

The sculpted stonework was painted in a Grey colour to which I have added a tiny touch of Sepia or Snakebite Leather to add some 'warmth' to the exposed stones. The groundcover was painted in my usual Snakebite Leather colour which was later drybrushed with a Snakebite/Titanium White highlight. The rocks and individual stones were painted Grey and highlighted with White .

## 5. DETAILS &amp; DOOR

The small details were picked out in various colours which were in turn inspired by a painting I had seen in an antique shop in Persore, Worcestershire.

The wooden door was painted in a well-worn Dark Blue and later an all-over wash of Light Tone helped to define the sculpting and particulary the exposed stone detail.

## 6. ASPIRE

The tiled roof was painted in a wide variety of different colours over a base of Linen and Titanium White craft paints making sure to allow the Linen and White to show through in the mortar gaps. Once dry the whole roof was given a light drybrush and then a wash of Soft Tone to 'age' the exposed mortar.

The small roof spire was actually constructed from a bamboo skewer as the original had broken off prior to me recieving the model. I have used Milliput to re-sculpt the metal flashing ( it'll probably be rather stronger. Ed . )

## 7 . PLA Y V ALUE

The main axle for the sails was superglued in place and the sails were painted with Linen craft paint (very appropriate), highlighted with both Cream and White . The wooden spars were painted in a Red Brown . I gave the inner sections of the fabric sails a very light wash.

The sails are not glued in place but can freely revolve on the resin axle. The conical roof has been modified to have three small lugs fitted to the inner lower edges so that it can freely turn on the main tower. The painted model is now able to be split into three: the main structure, the roof and the sails.

## 8. COATINGS

The model was varnished with a mix of Galleria Matt Varnish and matt ModPodge before the base was covered with a dyed sawdust and ground foam mix glued in place over PVA and then further decorated with static grass tufts and ground foam glued in place with superglue.

The windmill stands 230mm tall (to the top of the sail) and is based on a 130mm x 130mm plastic card base.

## 9. TRUMPTON AT WAR

The final image shows the finished Peninsular War Windmill on my small display gaming board with some 28mm Flintloque Ferach (French) alongside and shown for scale.

Although not my usual article or tutorial, I hope that this will give some inspiration and tips to anyone contemplating painting their own piece of Napoleonic themed wargame terrain.
